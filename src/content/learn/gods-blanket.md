# God's Blanket

"God's Blanket" is the FGW shorthand for the layer of mulch that covers
the soil at all times. Everywhere. It is the practical expression of
Key 5 (Cover the Soil) and the single most underrated investment in
the system.

## The Spec

- **Thickness**: {{thickness_cm}} cm, settled, across the whole
  cropped area.
- **Coverage**: **{{coverage_pct}}%**. No bare ground anywhere.

Settled is the operative word. Fresh straw fluffs to twice its
eventual depth — lay it 5 cm thick and let it rain once, and you have
roughly your {{thickness_cm}} cm.

## What Counts As Mulch

Anything carbonaceous, locally available, slow to decompose:

- Wheat, oat, or rye straw (cleanest; few weed seeds when threshed)
- Chopped leaves (drag a mower over fall leaf piles)
- Wood chips (best on perennials and paths; too slow on annual rows)
- Spent hay (rich, but watch for seed)
- Dried garden trimmings (free; rotates back through the system)

The chapter mulch drive (kind-31923 labor event, type
`mulch-drive`) is built around stockpiling these in late autumn and
spring.

## Why It Works

1. **Moisture**: a {{thickness_cm}} cm cap halves evaporation. Plants
   irrigate themselves from soil moisture instead of from the hose.
2. **Temperature**: the mulched root zone runs 5–10 °C cooler in
   summer and 3–5 °C warmer overnight. Roots love stable temperatures.
3. **Weeds**: most weed seeds need light to germinate. Mulch locks them
   out.
4. **Biology**: the mulch / soil interface is where decomposers thrive.
   Earthworms, fungi, springtails — they live where there is shade and
   moisture and carbon.
5. **Erosion**: rainfall hits the blanket, not the soil. The blanket
   spreads the impact and meters water in.

## When To Refresh

Whenever the mulch thins below about 1.5 cm settled, top up to
{{thickness_cm}} cm. In a humid temperate climate that is twice a
year — once in spring before planting, once in autumn before frost.

## When *Not* To Mulch

- The seedbed itself, until seedlings are up. Mulch goes around them
  once they have their first true leaves.
- Direct seeded carrots / parsnips for the first 2 weeks. They will
  push through if you must, but it is a struggle.

Otherwise, blanket everything.
