# Key 1 — On Time

Of the six management keys, "On Time" is the one most chapters get
wrong. Not because they are lazy — because life intervenes. The garden
does not care.

## Why Calendar Time Beats Vibe Time

Compost pile thermodynamics, plant phenology, and pest cycles are all
calendar-driven. A pile that should be turned on day 3 will lose 10 °C
of core temperature by day 5. A planting that should go in on April 15
will set fruit a week late if it goes in April 22, and that week
compounds across the season.

## What "On Time" Actually Means

- **Pile turns**: days 3, 6, 9, 19, 29, 39 from build. Not "around
  then." If day 6 is a Sunday and you cannot turn until Monday,
  Monday is wrong — it is day 7.
- **Planting**: per the local frost calendar and the variety's heat
  unit requirement. Save the date when you order seed, not when the
  seedlings come up.
- **Harvest**: at peak ripeness, not at convenience. Tomatoes left a
  day past ripe lose flavour; corn left a day past ripe goes starchy.
- **Seed save**: from the first plants of each variety to flower true
  to type — flag them on the day, not the week.

## How Chapters Stay On Time

1. **Shared calendar**: every chapter publishes a labor calendar
   (kind-31923 events). RSVP is binding.
2. **Notifications**: turn-day reminders fire at 09:00 local for every
   pile a member is subscribed to.
3. **Cross-cover**: if you cannot make a turn, post the conflict in the
   chapter chat 24 hours ahead. Someone else covers, and you owe them.
4. **No grace days**: missing one turn is a lesson. Missing two is a
   pile that will not finish hot.

## When You Slip

Slips happen. When they do:

- Note the slip in the pile's turn record (the app does this for you).
- Do the next turn on schedule, not after a "catch-up" turn.
- Expect a slightly cooler core for one cycle. The pile will recover.

The point is not perfection — it is honesty. A pile honestly turned 5
of 6 times is a pile that taught the chapter what slipping costs.
