# Compost Recipe

The FGW compost pile is a recipe, not a guess. Build it to spec and
turn it on time and you will reliably hit thermophilic temperatures
within a week.

## Reference Pile

A standard pile is **2 m × 2 m × 2 m**. It is built in **8 to 9
layers**, each one nominally **{{layer_total_cm}} cm** thick before
compaction.

## Per-Layer Recipe

Every complete layer (from the bottom up) is:

- **{{woody_cm}} cm woody** (chopped brush, small branches, wood chips)
- **{{dry_cm}} cm dry** (straw, dried leaves, dead plant matter)
- **{{green_cm}} cm green** (fresh leaves, garden trimmings, kitchen
  scraps)
- **{{manure_bags_50kg}} × 50 kg bags of manure**
- **{{water_min_l}}–{{water_max_l}} L water**, sprinkled across the
  layer

Stack 8 to 9 of these and you have a 2 m pile.

## Turn Schedule

Turn the pile on days **{{turn_day_1}}, {{turn_day_2}}, {{turn_day_3}},
{{turn_day_4}}, {{turn_day_5}}, {{turn_day_6}}** — counted from the
build day. The first three turns are close together to keep oxygen
moving while the pile is at peak biological activity; the last three
spread out as the pile matures.

A common shorthand: "every 3 days for the first three turns, then
every 10 days for the next 2 or 3 turns."

## Target Conditions

While turning, check:

- **Temperature**: {{temp_min_c}}–{{temp_max_c}} °C in the core. Below
  that, the pile is too cool — it needs more green or more turning.
  Above, it is going anaerobic — add water and turn early.
- **Moisture**: about **{{moisture_pct}}%** by the squeeze test. Squeeze
  a fistful: a few drops of water should bead at your knuckles. Soaking
  is too wet. Dust is too dry.

## Cure

After the sixth turn, let the pile cure undisturbed for **120 days**.
Cure is when fungal hyphae knit the finished compost together — skipping
cure leaves you with raw biology that will burn young roots.
