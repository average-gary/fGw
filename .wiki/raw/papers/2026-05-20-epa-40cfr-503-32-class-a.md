# 40 CFR §503.32 — Class A Pathogen Densities

The U.S. legal definition of "pathogen-free" compost, as a measurable density.

## The thresholds

- Fecal coliform: **<1000 MPN per gram of total solids (dry weight)**
- Salmonella: **<3 MPN per 4 g of total solids (dry weight)**

Class A status requires the compost to satisfy *one* of six Alternatives (1–6). Alternative 5 is "Use of a Process to Further Reduce Pathogens (PFRP)" — i.e., the windrow / static-pile / within-vessel thresholds in [Appendix B](2026-05-20-epa-40cfr-503-pfrp.md).

## Why ingested

- Class A is what STA Certified Compost (USCC) lab-tests confirm.
- Tells the wiki what "pathogen-free" *measures* as, not just what process *should* produce it.
- Important for any chapter-elder who wants to distribute compost beyond the chapter (CSA shareholders, neighbor gardens) and needs to know "is this OK?"

## FGW vs Class A — gap

FGW does **no microbial testing**. A PFRP-compliant FGW pile is *presumed* Class A by the EPA pathway-based logic but is never *measured* Class A. This is the same gap commercial home-composters face: process compliance ≠ outcome compliance.

For Powder Keg's own use (chapter members applying chapter compost on chapter-member fields), the regulatory framework doesn't bind. For *any distribution beyond chapter*, the chapter has a soft obligation to either (a) lab-test annually or (b) document PFRP compliance with an instrumented temperature log.
