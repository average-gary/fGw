# van Donk et al. 2011 — Wood chip mulch thickness effects

A randomized controlled trial in the *Journal of Applied Horticulture* directly comparing four mulch depths: 0 (bare soil), 2.5, 5, and 10 cm. The rare paper that puts the FGW 2.5 cm spec under the microscope as a study factor.

## Why ingested

- The single most relevant academic source for the round-2 assess research gap #16 (MULCH constants without published-benchmark anchor).
- Tests the 2.5 cm threshold *directly* against thicker alternatives, with statistical comparisons.
- Wood chip mulch is a reasonable proxy for the woody-plus-dry mulch mix the Field Guide implies.

## Key findings

- **Moisture conservation**: All mulched depths (2.5, 5, 10 cm) conserved soil water vs bare soil. **2.5 cm did not differ meaningfully from thicker applications for moisture conservation.**
- **Weed suppression**: Weeds at 2.5 cm were *statistically equivalent to bare soil*. **Only 5 and 10 cm suppressed weeds significantly.**
- **Soil temperature**: Mid-day soil temperature moderated at all mulched depths.
- **Plant growth**: Minor differences across treatments; 10 cm slightly delayed flowering by ~2 days (a hint at the spring-warming penalty captured in [Source #3 of this round](2026-05-20-spring-soil-warming-penalty.md)).

## Bearing on FGW's 2.5 cm prescription

**Mixed.** The depth is *defensible for soil-moisture conservation* (FGW's primary moisture-stress-mitigation rationale in southern Africa) but **inadequate for weed suppression**, which the Field Guide explicitly claims for the same 2.5 cm spec at line 968 ("Weeding is best done by smother mulching with supplementary blanket cover at 100% cover and 2,5cm thick"). If weed suppression is the *real* objective, the Field Guide's 2.5 cm number is too thin per van Donk's data — 5 cm is the empirical inflection point.

For the fGw app's Learn page, this means:
- The 2.5 cm constant is fine *as a moisture-conservation floor*.
- The Field Guide's claim that 2.5 cm = effective weed control is unsupported by this RCT. Either thicker mulch (5+ cm) is needed, or the chapter accepts that hand-weeding will still be required (as Giller et al. 2015 documents — hand-weeding labor under CA is "strongly increased").

## Limitations

- Wood chip mulch, not crop residue. Crop residue may pack denser and achieve weed suppression at thinner depths; van Donk 2011 doesn't test this.
- Landscape (ornamental) context, not row-crop. Field Guide context is maize.
- Single-site study; not a meta-analysis.
