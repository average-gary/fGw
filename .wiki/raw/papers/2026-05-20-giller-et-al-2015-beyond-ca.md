# Giller et al. 2015 — Beyond conservation agriculture

Open-access in *Frontiers in Plant Science*, DOI 10.3389/fpls.2015.00870. 667 citations as of mid-2026.

## Why ingested

- Largest peer-reviewed evidence base on conservation-agriculture outcomes (which FGW is a member of).
- Provides the climate-mismatch ammunition for any "do FGW constants transfer cleanly to USDA Zone 6b WV?" question.
- Documents that mulching ("God's Blanket") competes directly with livestock-feed in smallholder economies — relevant to chapters that include livestock keepers.

## Key findings

- **No-till alone yields a ~10% penalty overall**, averaged across the 5,463 paired observations.
- Yield benefits appear *only in dry climates* (sub-tropical, semi-arid).
- *In wetter regions tillage actually helps* by warming and drying the soil profile faster in the spring — no-till is counterproductive.
- Soil organic carbon gains under no-till are **not generic**; they require above-ground biomass production above a threshold, otherwise SOC is unchanged.
- Hand-weeding labor under CA is *strongly increased*, falling disproportionately on women.
- Smallholders prioritize feeding crop residues to livestock over leaving them as soil mulch — direct economic conflict with FGW's "blanket" doctrine.

## Bearing on fGw / Powder Keg

- Powder Keg WV is humid-continental, not semi-arid. Per this paper, the *yield-positive case for no-till is weakest in this climate.* The wiki's Learn page should not promise yield gains in WV that the literature doesn't support.
- FGW's compost spec is independent of the no-till/mulch core, so this paper does not contest the compost recipe. But it does call into question the broader "FGW package" claim.
