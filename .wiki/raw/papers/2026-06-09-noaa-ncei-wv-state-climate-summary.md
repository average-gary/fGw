# NOAA — West Virginia State Climate Summary

## Why ingested

Authoritative climate baseline for WV. Provides macro context for why historical frost dates may need a modern shift, and quantifies the precipitation regime FGW mulch interacts with.

## Key findings

- WV temperatures up ~1°F since early 1900s; warming trend since the 1980s.
- **Annual precipitation: 45.1″ (1895–2020)**; recent wet period **2015–2020 averaged 51.9″**.
- Mountainous regions ~100″ snow/year — relevant to elevation-zoned planting.
- Projected winter/spring precipitation increases and more extreme rainfall — affects FGW mulch + planting-window risk.
- Very cold nights below average since the 1990s — slightly earlier last-frost trend, supporting earlier-than-historical planting in panhandle / valleys.

## Notes for FGW calendar translation

- 45.1″ annual precip is **roughly half** the FGW posters' implicit southern-Africa-rainy-season concentration (which is more like 25–35″ falling Nov–Mar) but the SAME magnitude over a year — the difference is distribution, not total. The chapter article should make the distribution comparison explicit.
- The 51.9″ 2015–2020 wet period puts FGW's "100% mulch" prescription under more saturation pressure than the long-run baseline would suggest.
- Slug + slug + damping-off failure modes documented in the contrarian sources are amplified by this precipitation regime.
