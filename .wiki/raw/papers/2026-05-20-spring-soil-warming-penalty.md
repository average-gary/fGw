# Spring soil-warming penalty under heavy residue cover

The single most actionable contrarian finding for Powder Keg WV. The Field Guide's blanket prescription is anchored on southern-Africa rainy-season agronomy; in humid-continental climates, the same prescription costs emergence days every spring.

## Why ingested

- Field Guide is silent on spring soil-warming. This source fills the gap.
- Powder Keg WV (USDA Zone 6b) is on the cold-spring margin where the penalty is documented to be highest.
- Directly informs round-2 assess opportunity #3 ("Climate-adaptation banner" on Learn page) — the wiki now has a citable paper for that banner instead of just inferring from Giller 2015.

## Key findings

- **Mechanism**: "Residue reflects sunlight and insulates the soil, reducing both warming and drying of fields in the spring" (Pioneer agronomy summary, corroborated by U-Illinois Extension).
- **Effect**: Heavy residue cover causes "delayed and more variable emergence" of the following crop.
- **Geographic concentration**: "Managing residue to maximize soil warming and drying is especially challenging in the northern Corn Belt where cold and wet soils can delay sowing" — peer-reviewed Soil & Tillage Research framing.
- **Magnitude**: Continuous corn (where residue accumulates most) shows documented yield penalty despite occupying >20% of corn acres.
- **Quantitative shorthand**: practitioners cite "1 to 2 weeks" delay in planting window in the most affected northern fields.

## Bearing on FGW in WV (Zone 6b)

The Field Guide's southern-Africa origin means the manual was written for soils that warm reliably under the rainy-season planting calendar (Nov–Dec planting after first significant rains). Powder Keg WV operates on a different calendar:

- WV last-frost: typically late April / early May
- WV field-corn planting window: May, narrow
- A 2.5 cm uniform blanket every spring will:
  1. Delay soil-warming-to-planting-temperature by days
  2. Compress the planting window further
  3. Increase emergence variability for heat-loving crops (corn, beans, squash)

This is the **mirror failure mode** of [Giller et al. 2015](2026-05-20-giller-et-al-2015-beyond-ca.md) (no-till underperforms in *wetter* regions) — the climate-mismatch case. Together they argue: the FGW package was *calibrated for a specific climate* and yields net-negative when transplanted naively.

## Mitigations practitioners use (not in Field Guide)

From the Mid-Atlantic / Northeast extension consensus (PSU, U-Illinois, Cornell):

- **Strip-till the planting row only** — pull the blanket aside in a narrow strip 2 weeks before planting; lets the seed-row warm; preserves between-row coverage. Conflicts with FGW's "100% coverage" rule.
- **Vertical tillage in fall** — break heavy residue into smaller pieces that decompose faster; less insulating in spring. Conflicts with FGW's "no plough" rule.
- **Delay the blanket** — apply the full mulch *after* emergence, not before planting. The Field Guide actually points this direction at line 956–961: "leave a 5cm gap to allow for germination to take place" for non-maize crops. The wiki should surface this nuance and possibly extend the gap rule to all crops in cold-spring climates.
