# PSU Slug BMP — No-Till + Mulch Conflict

## Why ingested

The strongest land-grant statement of the conservation-vs-pest tradeoff in the Mid-Atlantic. PSU's geography (Zone 6a–6b, Allegheny Plateau) is the closest analog to WV.

## Key findings

- "In field crops, slugs are particularly prevalent in **no-till or reduced-till fields with heavy residue and little soil disturbance**."
- "This damage tends to be most severe under **cool, wet conditions**, which slow crop growth and favor slug activity." (= classic WV April–May)
- **BMP**: plant when soil reaches 60–70°F for corn — i.e., delay until the seedbed warms.
- Row cleaners removing residue from the seed row contradict 100% mulch.
- Explicit statement: "Most growers who experience slug problems are committed to no-till or reduced-till practices, so while tillage will certainly help control slugs, it may not be an option."

## Notes for FGW calendar translation

- The 60–70°F-soil planting gate is the cleanest single calendar argument the chapter article has against the FGW posters' rainfall-onset trigger: in WV, **planting must wait for the seedbed temperature**, not just calendar date or rain pattern.
- The "cool wet" failure-window matches exactly the WV April–May conditions where the FGW package is most likely to disappoint.
