# Growing Green Beans in West Virginia

## Why ingested

WV-resident authority on bean culture. The 60°F soil-temp gate and the 110-day dry-bean number both constrain Zone 6b planting windows — the wiki needs both for honest calendar translation against the FGW posters.

## Key findings

- **Soil temperature for germination**: 60°F minimum. Below this, germination is poor and seed rot dominates.
- **Frost-intolerant**: any frost destroys the crop; cold soil → poor germination + seed rot (the empirical face of the [mulch-spring-warming-penalty](../../wiki/concepts/mulch-spring-warming-penalty.md) for legumes).
- **Days to maturity**:
  - Snap (bush): 50–65 d
  - Pole snap: up to 70 d
  - **Dry beans: up to 110 d**
- **Succession**: every 2–3 weeks.
- **Pod set**: ≈ 2 weeks from flowering.
- **Late-June planting trick**: used in WV to dodge Mexican bean beetle generations.

## Notes for FGW calendar translation

- 60°F at 4 inches in WV typically arrives **mid-May** for valley loam; **late May** for hilltop / clay soils — consistent with VCE 6b May 5 – Jun 25 snap window, but pushed later for cooler microclimates.
- **Dry bean window is tight in 6b**: a 110-day dry-bean planted May 15 finishes Sept 2; planted Jun 15 finishes Oct 3 — the latter cuts very close to first-frost (median ≈ Oct 10–15 in 6b lowlands). Variety selection matters; **Cherokee Trail of Tears (~85 d), Black Turtle (~80–90 d), Good Mother Stallard (~85 d)** are all safer than 110-day cultivars.
- The Mexican bean beetle delay-trick is an Appalachian adaptation worth flagging in the calendar article — FGW posters know nothing of it.
