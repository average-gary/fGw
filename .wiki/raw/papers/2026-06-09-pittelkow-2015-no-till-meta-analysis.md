# Pittelkow et al. 2015 — Productivity Limits of Conservation Agriculture

## Why ingested

The canonical peer-reviewed counter-evidence to the FGW assumption that "no-till + 100% mulch always raises yield." The dry-climate finding is decisive for the chapter calendar article: FGW's home turf is dry-tropical; **WV is humid-temperate, the worst quadrant for the package as configured.**

## Key findings

- Meta-analysis: 5,463 paired observations, 48 crops, 63 countries.
- "Overall, our results show that no-till reduces yields, yet this response is variable."
- "When no-till is combined with the other two conservation agriculture principles of residue retention and crop rotation, its negative impacts are minimized."
- No-till + full CA package boosts rainfed yield in **dry climates** specifically; **humid temperate is where the penalty bites**.
- Authors caution against expansion in smallholder systems where the full package can't be maintained.
