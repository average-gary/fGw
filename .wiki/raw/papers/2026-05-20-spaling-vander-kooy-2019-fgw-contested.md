# Spaling & Vander Kooy 2019 — Farming God's Way: agronomy and faith contested

Published in *Agriculture and Human Values* (Springer), DOI 10.1007/s10460-019-09925-2. Paywalled; ingested via abstract + Scholar snippets.

## Why ingested

- Highest-quality peer-reviewed source naming FGW directly.
- Explicitly engages the theological framing — i.e., the practitioner stance that ratios and turn cadences are doctrinally fixed.
- Documents *agronomic friction*: "early weed competition and reduced yield" as adoption barriers.
- Useful counterweight when the wiki documents `src/domain/fgw.ts` constants. The Field Guide is the *what*; this paper is the *how-defensible-is-this*.

## Findings (from abstract + snippets)

- FGW conflates biblical authority with prescriptive agronomic numbers; that conflation is itself a methodological weakness, not a feature.
- Adoption barriers identified: weed pressure during the no-till transition years, labor shifts that fall on women, dependence on supplemental inputs (hybrid seed + fertilizer) that confound "FGW package" yield claims.
- Co-authors are sympathetic to the Christian-agriculture context (Spaling at King's University; Vander Kooy with practitioner background) — this is critique from inside the tent, not from outside.

## Limitation

- Body paywalled. Wiki article should cite this paper for the framing claim and quote the abstract verbatim, not invent body content.
