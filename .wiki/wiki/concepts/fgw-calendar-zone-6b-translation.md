# FGW calendar — Zone 6b WV translation

The chapter-side companion to the [FGW Master Sequence Posters](../../raw/papers/2026-05-20-fgw-master-sequence-posters.md). The posters anchor on the southern-Africa summer-rainfall season (planting after first significant rains, Nov–Dec). Powder Keg WV is humid-continental USDA Zone 6b (with 5b–7a edges across elevation). This article translates each FGW operation onto a Zone 6b WV calendar, citing extension and practitioner data, and flags the climate-mismatch failure modes the southern-Africa calendar doesn't surface.

## What the FGW posters anchor on (vs WV)

| Axis | FGW posters (southern Africa) | Zone 6b WV |
|---|---|---|
| Climate type | Tropical / sub-tropical, summer rainfall | Humid continental |
| Limiting factor at planting | **Moisture** — wait for first significant rains | **Soil temperature** — wait for sustained warming |
| Planting trigger | Rain onset (typ. Nov–Dec) | Soil ≥ 55–60°F at 4 in (typ. late April → mid May) |
| Annual precip pattern | Concentrated Nov–Mar (~25–35 in) | 45.1 in averaged over the year (51.9 in 2015–2020) |
| Mulch role | Retain just-arrived moisture | Insulate against cold-spring soil warming |
| Frost risk | None | Median last frost mid-April → mid-May; first frost early-mid October |

The implication: **the FGW calendar's "rain onset" trigger is the wrong gate in WV.** The seedbed-temperature gate is the right one. Sources: [FGW Master Sequence Posters](../../raw/papers/2026-05-20-fgw-master-sequence-posters.md), [NOAA WV state climate summary](../../raw/papers/2026-06-09-noaa-ncei-wv-state-climate-summary.md), [Penn State slug BMP](../../raw/papers/2026-06-09-penn-state-slug-no-till-bmps.md), [WVU green beans](../../raw/papers/2026-06-09-wvu-extension-growing-green-beans-wv.md).

## WV zone reference (2026)

Per the [USDA 2023 Plant Hardiness Zone Map update](../../raw/papers/2026-06-09-usda-2023-hardiness-zone-map-release.md), West Virginia spans:

| Zone | Where | Last-frost (median) | First-frost (median) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5b | Highest elevations (>3,500 ft); Pocahontas, Tucker, Randolph high country | Late May | Late September |
| 6a | Mountain valleys; ~1,500–3,000 ft | Mid May | Early October |
| **6b** | **Most of the state — Charleston, Morgantown, Lewisburg, mid-elevation** | **Late April – Early May** | **Mid October** |
| 7a | Eastern panhandle (Berkeley, Jefferson); deep southern lowlands (Mingo, Logan) | Mid–Late April | Late October |

The 1991–2020 30-year-minimum average is **+3.3 °F warmer** than the 1976–2005 average ([USDA 2023](../../raw/papers/2026-06-09-usda-2023-hardiness-zone-map-release.md), [NPR mapping](../../raw/papers/2026-06-09-climate-central-2025-growing-season-trends.md) corroborates regional pattern). [Climate Central 2025](../../raw/papers/2026-06-09-climate-central-2025-growing-season-trends.md) puts the **Ohio Valley** (covering western/southern WV) at 188 average frost-free days and the **Northeast** (eastern panhandle, mountains) at 180 days — both up by 13 and 17 days respectively since 1970.

## Operation-by-operation translation

The FGW Master Sequence Posters' rows: **land prep → compost build → compost application → planting → weeding → top-dressing → harvest → post-harvest**. Each row below is the WV calendar equivalent for **maize and dry beans** in Zone 6b lowlands (panhandle / Greenbrier / Charleston). For Zone 6a / 5b mountain-county adjustment: **add 1–2 weeks**. For Zone 7a panhandle / deep-south lowlands: **subtract 1 week**.

### 1. Land prep

| Window | Operation |
|---|---|
| **Late Feb – early Apr** | Mark planting stations on contour; check drainage; remove woody weeds. Cold-saturated soil — do not work it; stay off it on rainy days. |
| **Late Mar – mid Apr** | Pull mulch back from row strips ~10–14 days before target plant date so the seedbed warms (per [mulch spring-warming penalty](mulch-spring-warming-penalty.md)). The [UMD 2022 slug IPM](../../raw/papers/2026-06-09-umd-ext-2022-slug-ipm.md) bulletin recommends a **10–12 in cleared strip** for the seed row. |

The FGW posters' "land prep" row assumes a long dry season and rainfall-cued breaking; WV's analog is **frost-cleared-and-warming** soil, not a rain trigger.

### 2. Compost build

| Window | Operation |
|---|---|
| **Sep – Nov (preceding year)** | Start the FGW pile from the previous season's residues + manure + soil. Cold-temperature curing slows microbial activity but the pile finishes by spring. |
| **Jan – Feb** | Optional second pile build using over-wintered carbon sources (corn stover, wood chips, autumn leaves) for late-spring crops. |

[Pam Dawling at Twin Oaks (VA Zone 7a)](../../raw/papers/2026-06-09-dawling-twin-oaks-task-list-may.md) demonstrates the winter-pile-to-spring-application cycle. Screened compost is stored under cover (greenhouse, hoop, tarped pile) so it stays unfrozen and ready by mid-January for first seedlings, mid-April for first crop applications. The wiki's [pile-build process article](fgw-pile-build-process.md) covers FGW pile mechanics independent of season.

### 3. Compost application

| Window | Operation |
|---|---|
| **Mid Mar – mid Apr** | Apply finished compost into planting stations on a 2-week-or-more buffer before plant date. Spring-warming benefits: dark surface absorbs heat faster than mulch; nitrogen mineralization synchronizes with crop uptake (per [PSU PSNT](../../raw/papers/2026-06-09-psu-extension-nitrogen-fertilization-corn-psnt.md)). |

This corresponds to the FGW posters' "compost application" row — but it must precede the seedbed-warming window, not coincide with rain onset.

### 4. Planting

The single-most-important translation. **Plant by soil temperature, not date.**

| Crop | Soil-temp gate | Zone 6b first plant date | Zone 6b last plant date | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sweet corn (su standard) | 55°F at 4 in | **Apr 25** | **Jul 10** | [VCE](../../raw/papers/2026-06-09-vce-426-331-virginia-vegetable-planting-dates.md): Apr 20 – Jul 10. [WVU](../../raw/papers/2026-06-09-wvu-extension-growing-sweet-corn-wv.md): mid-late April → mid July. |
| Sweet corn (se / sh2) | 60°F at 4 in | **May 5** | **Jul 5** | Stricter gate; pushes first planting back ~10 d. |
| Field corn (FGW maize equivalent) | 50°F at 4 in (silver minimum); 55°F preferred | **Apr 25 – May 10** | **Jun 10** (so ~110-day variety finishes before frost) | Variety-dependent. WV's typical late-April / early-May first-corn-planting matches northern PA practice ([PSU](../../raw/papers/2026-06-09-psu-sweet-corn-production.md)). |
| Snap / pole bean | 60°F at 4 in (WVU); 55°F (PSU) | **May 5 – May 15** | **Jul 25** (snap fall sow) / **Jul 1** (pole) | [VCE 6b](../../raw/papers/2026-06-09-vce-426-331-virginia-vegetable-planting-dates.md): snap May 5 – Jun 25; pole May 15 – Jun 15. WVU's 60°F is the conservative gate. |
| Dry bean (≤90 d variety) | 60°F at 4 in | **May 10 – Jun 1** | **Jun 15** | At 110 d the WV window shrinks to nothing; **prefer ≤90 d cultivars** like Cherokee Trail of Tears, Black Turtle. See [WVU heritage beans](../../raw/papers/2026-06-09-wvu-extension-heritage-beans.md). |
| Dry bean (>100 d variety) | 60°F at 4 in | **May 5** | **May 25** | Tight; first-frost finish requires earliest possible plant date. |

Soil-temperature → days-to-germination from [Pam Dawling's data](../../raw/papers/2026-06-09-dawling-sweet-corn-succession.md):

| Soil temp at 4 in | Days to corn germination |
|---|---|
| 50°F | 22 |
| 59°F | 12 |
| 68°F | 7 |
| 77°F | 4 |

That's a **5× spread** across the realistic spring soil-temperature range — the strongest single argument for the soil-temp gate over a calendar-date gate.

**Phenology cues for chapter members without a soil thermometer:**

- "Oak leaves as big as squirrel's ears" → soil ~55–60°F → corn-plant-go ([Dawling](../../raw/papers/2026-06-09-dawling-sweet-corn-succession.md)).
- "Dogwood petals dropping" → soil ~70°F → bean-plant-go (warm-soil crops).

### 5. Succession

FGW posters anchor on a single planting; WV market-garden practice anchors on succession.

**Sweet corn succession (Zone 6b WV, 6 plantings, derived from [Dawling Zone 7a +14 d](../../raw/papers/2026-06-09-dawling-sweet-corn-succession.md)):**

| Planting | Date | Days from previous |
|---|---|---|
| #1 | May 10 | — |
| #2 | Jun 2 | 23 |
| #3 | Jun 20 | 18 |
| #4 | Jul 8 | 18 |
| #5 | Jul 21 | 13 |
| #6 | Jul 30 | 9 |

Last planting (#6) is **tight against first frost in mountain WV** — drop it for Zones 6a / 5b.

**Snap-bean succession**: every 2–3 weeks ([WVU](../../raw/papers/2026-06-09-wvu-extension-growing-green-beans-wv.md), [PSU](../../raw/papers/2026-06-09-psu-snap-bean-production.md)) through August in lowland WV; through mid-July in 6a; through end of June in 5b.

**Late-June bean planting** is an Appalachian trick to dodge the first Mexican Bean Beetle generation ([WVU green beans](../../raw/papers/2026-06-09-wvu-extension-growing-green-beans-wv.md)) — not on the FGW posters but worth a chapter addendum.

### 6. Weeding

The FGW posters anchor weeding on a fixed cadence after planting. WV practice ([Dawling Zone 7a](../../raw/papers/2026-06-09-dawling-sweet-corn-succession.md)):

| Days after planting | Operation |
|---|---|
| 7 | First hoe / weed pass |
| 14 | Second hoe; corn at 6–12 in |
| 21 | Third hoe; corn at 12 in |
| 28–35 | Final cultivation; undersow cover crop. **Stop cultivating after corn is knee-high (~18–20 in)** — root damage > weed cost. |

Compatible with FGW's "no plough" principle if cultivation is shallow (top inch only).

### 7. Top-dressing

The FGW posters' "top-dressing" row maps to **sidedress nitrogen** in WV extension language. Per [PSU PSNT](../../raw/papers/2026-06-09-psu-extension-nitrogen-fertilization-corn-psnt.md), corn N uptake is minimal until ~35 days after emergence, then a sharp uptake spurt — sidedress matches demand.

| Crop | Stage | Calendar (Zone 6b, May 5 plant) | Operation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Field corn | V4–V6 (10–20 in tall) | Memorial Day → mid-June | Sidedress N — half of total N applied here. PSNT sample at V4 (12 in tall) one week ahead. |
| Sweet corn | 4–5 fully expanded leaves (V4–V5) | Late May → early June | Per [WVU](../../raw/papers/2026-06-09-wvu-extension-growing-sweet-corn-wv.md): half N pre-plant + half at this stage. |
| Sweet corn (PSU) | 24 in tall | First-third week of June | [PSU](../../raw/papers/2026-06-09-psu-sweet-corn-production.md) — slightly later than the WVU rule; either is defensible. |
| Beans | First flower | ~6 weeks post-plant | Light N or compost top-up; beans fix their own N once nodulated. |

### 8. Harvest

| Crop | Days to harvest from plant | Zone 6b harvest window (May plant) |
|---|---|---|
| Sweet corn (su) | ~75 d total; ~17–24 d after first silk ([VCE](../../raw/papers/2026-06-09-vce-426-331-virginia-vegetable-planting-dates.md)) | Mid-July → early October |
| Snap bean (bush) | 50–65 d ([WVU](../../raw/papers/2026-06-09-wvu-extension-growing-green-beans-wv.md)) | Late June → mid-October |
| Pole bean | up to 70 d | Mid-July → mid-October |
| Dry bean (≤90 d cv) | ~85–90 d to dry pods | Mid-August → late September |
| Dry bean (110 d cv) | ~110 d | Mid-September → mid-October — *cuts close to first-frost in 6b mountain* |

[Dawling](../../raw/papers/2026-06-09-dawling-sweet-corn-succession.md): last sowing date = first-frost − days-to-maturity − 7-to-14-day harvest window.

### 9. Post-harvest

| Window | Operation |
|---|---|
| **Late Sep – Oct** | Pull dry-bean plants for threshing; cut corn stalks for silage / compost / mulch. |
| **Oct – Nov** | Apply finishing layer of mulch — *now* the FGW "100% blanket" is fully appropriate in WV (no soil-warming penalty post-harvest; mulch protects against winter freeze-thaw and erosion). |
| **Oct – Nov** | Begin building next year's compost pile from this year's residue + autumn leaves + manure. |

This is the operation where the **FGW calendar and the WV calendar converge**: post-harvest mulch + compost build are climate-agnostic.

## Failure modes the FGW calendar doesn't surface

The southern-Africa-anchored FGW posters do not flag any of these. The chapter article must:

1. **Cold-wet seedbed → slugs.** [PSU](../../raw/papers/2026-06-09-penn-state-slug-no-till-bmps.md) and [UMD 2022 IPM](../../raw/papers/2026-06-09-umd-ext-2022-slug-ipm.md) document slugs as no-till + heavy-residue + cool-wet-spring failure mode. WV April–May is exactly this regime. **Mitigation**: 10–12 in row clear-strip ([UMD](../../raw/papers/2026-06-09-umd-ext-2022-slug-ipm.md)) compatible with FGW if applied as a temporary planting-row pull-back, mulch reapplied after emergence.

2. **Cold-wet seedbed → damping-off.** Penn State turfgrass extension explicitly names "water-logged soils, high rates of fertilizer and mulch" as damping-off conditions — same regime, pathogen vector instead of pest. **Mitigation**: same as slugs (warm seedbed, narrow clear-strip, planting-temp gate).

3. **Mulch + canopy cover → voles.** [MU G4448](../../raw/papers/2026-06-09-mu-ext-g4448-vole-no-till.md) reports up to **80–100% stand reductions** in dense vole populations in no-till. Voles need full canopy cover; FGW's "God's blanket" is the habitat. **Mitigation menu (no-till compatible)**: perimeter mowing, raptor perches, snake habitat, predator presence (cats/dogs), fall mulch removal in vole-pressured fields.

4. **Spring soil-warming penalty.** Already documented in the wiki at [mulch-spring-warming-penalty.md](mulch-spring-warming-penalty.md). The 100% mulch blanket reflects sunlight (albedo) and insulates (air gap), losing the planting window 1–2 weeks. **Mitigation**: apply blanket *after emergence*; FGW Field Guide already specifies a 5 cm gap for non-maize crops, the chapter extends the rule to all crops.

5. **False-spring damage.** [WV MetroNews 2025](../../raw/papers/2026-06-09-wv-metronews-2025-late-frost-drought.md) reports late-spring 2025 frost damaged WV vegetable plantings statewide. **Mitigation**: even with warmer 30-year averages, target the **50% probability last-frost** date as the earliest plant date and the **10% probability** date as the earliest *risk-tolerant* plant date. Chapter members with no spare seed plant by 10% line.

6. **Climate-mismatch yield penalty.** [Pittelkow et al. 2015 (Nature)](../../raw/papers/2026-06-09-pittelkow-2015-no-till-meta-analysis.md) — meta-analysis: humid temperate is the worst climate quadrant for no-till, even with full residue + rotation. **Mitigation**: honest scope. The chapter article's job is not to prove FGW wrong, but to keep the package operational by shifting the calendar correctly.

## Confidence

**High** for the planting-window dates and soil-temp gates: triangulated across [VCE 426-331](../../raw/papers/2026-06-09-vce-426-331-virginia-vegetable-planting-dates.md), [WVU](../../raw/papers/2026-06-09-wvu-extension-growing-sweet-corn-wv.md), [PSU](../../raw/papers/2026-06-09-psu-sweet-corn-production.md), [UMD](../../raw/papers/2026-06-09-modern-frontierswoman-zone-6b-spring.md) (via the bracketing zone-6b sources), and two practitioner sites in Zone 7a and Zone 6b.

**High** for the failure-mode list: each is an independent peer-reviewed extension or Nature publication.

**Medium** for the per-elevation ±1–2-week adjustments and the compost-pile timing windows: extension publications give zone-level guidance but rarely WV-elevation-segmented calendars. The notification triggers are best-fit defaults for chapter use; chapter members should override based on local soil-thermometer readings.
