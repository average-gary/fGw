# FGW compost spec — academic standing & contested context

A balanced view of how the FGW Field Guide compost numbers stand in relation to peer-reviewed agronomy. Important context for the fGw app's Learn pages and for the Powder Keg chapter's calibration of expectations.

## Headline finding

**No peer-reviewed paper validates the FGW Field Guide compost-pile constants** (2 × 2 × 2 m, 6-turn schedule at days 3/6/9/19/29/39, ratios 10/45/22.5/22.5, 50% moisture, 55–68 °C, 120-day cure) against an independent comparator. The constants are **practitioner-codified tradition** descended from Brian Oldreive's experience scaling Hinton Estate (Zimbabwe) from 2 to 1000 ha — captured in the Field Guide and Trainer's Reference Guide.

Sources searched (Phase 1 academic agent): three Google-Scholar discovery queries and targeted abstract fetches across Springer, Frontiers, Taylor & Francis, ScienceDirect, ResearchGate, plus two MSc theses. The FGW-affiliated trials that exist measure **mulch + intercropping + no-till**, never the compost pile parameters.

## What the academic literature does engage with

The peer-reviewed critique of FGW clusters at the **system / framework level**, not the compost-recipe level:

### Spaling & Vander Kooy 2019 — *Agriculture and Human Values*
[Source](../../raw/papers/2026-05-20-spaling-vander-kooy-2019-fgw-contested.md). The single most-cited paper engaging FGW by name. Frames an "agronomy-vs-faith" tension. Documents adoption barriers: early weed competition, reduced yield in the no-till transition years, labor shifts that fall on women, dependence on supplemental inputs (hybrid seed + fertilizer) that confound "FGW package" yield claims. **Crucial for the wiki**: argues the conflation of biblical authority with prescriptive agronomic numbers is itself a methodological weakness.

> The fGw SPEC.md § 2.2 says: *"These constants are sacred."* That stance is exactly what Spaling & Vander Kooy contest.

### Giller, Andersson, Corbeels, Kirkegaard et al. 2015 — *Frontiers in Plant Science*
[Source](../../raw/papers/2026-05-20-giller-et-al-2015-beyond-ca.md). Largest meta-analysis of conservation-agriculture outcomes (5,463 paired observations). Finds:
- No-till alone yields a ~**10% penalty overall**.
- Yield benefits appear *only* in dry climates.
- *In wetter regions tillage actually helps* by warming and drying soils — no-till is counterproductive.
- Soil organic-C gains require above-threshold biomass production; they're not generic.
- Hand-weeding labor under CA is *strongly increased*, falling on women.
- Smallholders prioritize feeding crop residues to livestock over soil mulching.

**Powder Keg WV is humid-continental, not semi-arid.** Per this paper, Powder Keg is on the *climate-mismatched* side for the no-till/mulch core of FGW. (The compost spec is independent of the no-till core, so this paper does not contest the compost recipe — but it does call into question the broader "FGW package" claim that the Learn page should not over-promise.)

### Andersson & Giller 2012 — *Contested Agronomy*
[Source](../../raw/papers/2026-05-20-andersson-giller-2012-heretics.md). Names the FGW vocabulary directly ("God's blanket salesmen"). Argues the political/promotional machinery (FAO, DFID, EU, NGO) propagates FGW-style claims faster than agronomic evidence supports them. "Adoption" rates are inflated by donor incentives that collapse when subsidies end.

## What's not contested

The **compost recipe and turn schedule themselves**:
- No peer-reviewed source disputes the 10/45/22.5/22.5 ratio.
- No peer-reviewed source disputes the 6-turn / 39-day schedule.
- No peer-reviewed source disputes the 55–68 °C target or the 50% moisture target.

These look reasonable when sanity-checked against generic compost science (Cornell C:N work, Rynk's *On-Farm Composting Handbook*, Berkeley 18-day method). The 10% manure / 67.5% carbon split puts C:N somewhere in the 25–40 range, which is the target band. The 36-day high-temperature window with 6 turns satisfies [40 CFR §503 Appendix B PFRP](../../raw/papers/2026-05-20-epa-40cfr-503-pfrp.md) windrow on paper. See: [FGW pile regimen satisfies PFRP windrow](../theses/fgw-pile-satisfies-pfrp.md).

The thing that *is* unverifiable as written is the **measurement instrumentation**: FGW's "8 mm steel rod for 5 seconds" hand-test for temperature is not [TMECC](../../raw/papers/2026-05-20-fgw-field-guide-2nd-edition.md)-defensible. So a chapter pile may be **regimen-compliant** but not **measurement-compliant**.

## How the wiki should phrase this in the app

The Learn page (`src/content/learn/compost-recipe.md`) currently presents FGW numbers as authoritative. That's *fine* for a chapter following the practice — they're following Brian Oldreive's tradition, which is what they signed up for. But the wiki should:

1. **Cite sources honestly**: link the Learn page to [FGW Field Guide compost spec — verbatim reference](../references/fgw-field-guide-compost-spec.md), which traces every constant to a Field Guide page.
2. **Avoid overpromising yield**: don't make the Learn page claim FGW *outperforms* alternatives in WV — the literature doesn't support that for humid-continental climates.
3. **Honor the framework**: FGW is faith-rooted; that's the project's choice. The wiki documents it without ironizing it.

## Open questions (research gaps)

1. **Validate FGW pile temperature curves vs PFRP** with a calibrated probe over a season. Currently a thesis under [satisfies PFRP windrow](../theses/fgw-pile-satisfies-pfrp.md).
2. **Compare FGW finished-compost C:N and maturity (Solvita)** against Berkeley 18-day and Indore static. Nobody appears to have done this.
3. **Cold-climate adaptations** — Field Guide is southern-Africa-rainy-season-anchored. What changes for Zone 6b WV? Brian Smith (AgriStewards, Indiana, Zone 5b–6a) is the closest US peer; outreach pending.
4. **Vegetable-bed ratios** — explicitly different from maize-scale; live in the [Vegetable Guide](../../raw/papers/2026-05-20-fgw-vegetable-guide-2nd-edition.md), not yet ingested verbatim.
